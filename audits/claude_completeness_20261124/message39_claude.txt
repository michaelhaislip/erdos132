## Checks of the points you asked about

**(1) Conductor descent and odd kernel (doc §1).** Correct.
- **σ₂(A) identity.** σ₂(C) = C² − 2 and C² = A − 8 give σ₂(A) = A² − 20A + 108. Common prime divisors of A and σ₂(A) must divide 108 = 2²·3³.
- **Valuations at q ≥ 5.** Because A·σ₂(A) is a square in F, any prime of F above q ≥ 5 with v(A) > 0 has v(σ₂A) = 0, so v(A) is even. This holds whether or not q | e.
- **At 2.** The reduction of 2x² + Cx − 1 is Cx − 1 with C a unit, so its root is C⁻¹. The derivative 4x + C ≡ C is a unit, so Hensel applies. Then (C + 4x)² = C² + 8(Cx + 2x²) = C² + 8.
- **At 3 when 3 ∤ e.** A − δ² = 12, so A ≡ δ² ≢ 0 at every prime above 3.
- **Inertia components.** For E = ℚ(ζ_M), the factor (ℤ/q^{v_q M})* is the inertia group at q. When L/ℚ is unramified at q, that factor fixes L. Removing those factors leaves N with e | N and exactly the prime support of e (N is odd).
- **Odd kernel.** |P| = N/e is odd. P = Gal(ℚ(ζ_N)/K) ⊂ J and [J:H] ≤ 2, so P ⊂ H and L ⊂ K. Total reality then gives L ⊂ F.

**(2) β integrality and ππ̄ = 3.** Correct.
- β² − Cβ = (A − C²)/4 = 2, so β is an algebraic integer.
- π̄ = β − ζ⁻¹ because β ∈ F. Then ππ̄ = β² − Cβ + 1 = 3.
- K is CM, so τ(π̄) is the complex conjugate of τ(π) for every embedding τ. Hence every conjugate of π has absolute value √3.

**(3) Choice of a.** Correct.
- **3 ∤ e:** a = 3 is the Frobenius at 3, and 3 ≢ ±1 mod e when e ≥ 5.
- **3 | e, the choice of c:**
  - a = 1 + c·e/3 ≡ 1 mod f, where e = 3^k·f with 3 ∤ f.
  - If k ≥ 2, then a ≡ 1 mod 3. If k = 1, picking c·f ≡ 1 mod 3 gives a ≡ 2 mod 3. Either way gcd(a, e) = 1.
  - ζ^{ce/3} is a primitive cube root of unity.
- **3 | e, inertia:** σ_a ∈ Gal(K/ℚ(ζ_f)), which is exactly the inertia group at 3. In an abelian extension it fixes every prime above 3.
- **3 | e, a ≢ ±1:** a ≢ 1 because c·e/3 ≢ 0 mod e. a ≡ −1 would force ζ⁶ = 1. For e = 3 the only candidate is a = 2 ≡ −1, which is correctly excluded.

**(4) ε, its sign, and σ₂.** Correct.
